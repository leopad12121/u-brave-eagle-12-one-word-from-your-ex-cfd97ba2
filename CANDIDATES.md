# Candidates — One Word From Your Ex

Gate: 1–24 characters, exactly one word. Judge criteria, most important
first: (1) one word an ex would use to describe you; (2) makes the judge
laugh out loud, through surprise or a sharp turn; (3) stings with
recognition, painfully true and plainly known; (4) lands instantly, one word
that needs no explanation; (5) feels relatable, the kind of word people
repeat because they have lived it too.

## The 8 candidates (all composed fresh in this session)

1. "Exhausting" (11 chars) — Score 19/25. Painfully honest and funny, but a
   familiar complaint; many exes would say it.
2. "Intense" (7 chars) — Score 16/25. A classic ex word, but so common it
   carries no surprise.
3. "Unfinished" (10 chars) — Score 19/25. Stings — a person as unfinished
   business — but it leans sad more than funny.
4. "Project" (7 chars) — Score 18/25. "You were a project" is brutal and
   funny, but it needs the sentence around it to land.
5. "Temporary" (9 chars) — Score 17/25. Stings, but it describes the
   relationship more than the person.
6. "Placeholder" (11 chars) — Score 20/25. Brutal, funny, relatable — "you
   were a placeholder" — a close second, but it is a noun about a role, and
   it needs a beat to picture.
7. "Complicated" (11 chars) — Score 14/25. The word every ex reaches for
   first; no edge left in it.
8. "Almost" (6 chars) — Score 22/25. "You were almost." The whole failed
   relationship in one word: almost good enough, almost the one, almost
   there. It lands instantly, it stings because it is true, and no near
   synonym keeps its edge. Strongest of the eight.

## Pick

Candidate 8 — "Almost". It is the only candidate where the joke is what the
word does not say: the sentence it leaves unfinished is the sting, and every
reader fills it in themself.
